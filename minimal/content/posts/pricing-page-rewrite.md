Our pricing page has been rewritten three times in eighteen months.
Each version had a clear theory behind it, and each theory was at least
partly wrong. This is what we learned from the two that failed and the
one that is working now.

## Version one: three tiers, feature checklists

The first version looked like every SaaS pricing page you have seen:
three columns, a checklist of ticks and crosses, a "most popular"
ribbon on the middle tier. It converted reasonably well from paid
traffic, badly from organic.

The problem was not the layout. It was that the checklist asked a
visitor to evaluate features before they understood what the product
actually did for them. A tick next to "advanced scheduling" means
nothing to someone who has not yet decided scheduling matters.

## Version two: usage-based, no tiers

We tried removing tiers entirely and pricing by usage instead, with a
calculator. This solved the checklist problem but created a new one:
nobody could answer "how much will this cost me" without doing maths
first, and most visitors left rather than do the maths.

## Version three: one plan, a clear story

The current page has one plan, one price, and a short paragraph above
the price explaining what it replaces. No comparison table. A founder
looking at it should be able to decide in under a minute whether it is
for them, because there is only one decision to make.

Conversion from organic traffic roughly doubled against version one.
We cannot prove the page alone caused that, other things changed in the
same quarter, but it is the only page of the three visitors mention
unprompted in onboarding calls.

## What we kept across all three

A few things survived every rewrite, because they were never the part
that was wrong:

- A visible refund policy, stated plainly, not linked to a separate page.
- No "contact us for pricing" unless the plan genuinely needs a
  conversation (ours does not, so we never added it back).
- The price itself never changed. Only how we explained it did.

If your pricing page is not converting, our experience says to check
the story before the layout. We changed the layout twice before we
changed the story, and the story was the fix.
