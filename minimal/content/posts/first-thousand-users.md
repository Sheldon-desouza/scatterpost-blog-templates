Everyone asks the same question once they hear we crossed a thousand
users without a marketing budget: what actually worked? The honest
answer is a handful of unglamorous channels, in roughly this order.

## Launch day: a rounding error

We did the launch post, the forum threads, the whole routine. It
brought in under thirty signups, most of whom never came back. If you
are counting on launch day, lower your expectations now: it is a
starting gun, not a growth channel.

## Weeks two to six: one person at a time

The actual unlock was slower and less exciting: replying personally to
every signup's first support question, often within the hour, often
with a short screen recording rather than a wall of text. People told
their own teams about a tool that answered them like a human within
minutes. That word of mouth brought in roughly two hundred of the
first thousand.

## Month three: the comparison pages

We wrote honest comparison pages against the two tools we most often
replaced. Honest meaning we also listed what the other tool did
better. These pages ranked faster than anything else on the site and
brought steady, if unspectacular, organic traffic that is still growing.

## What did not work

- Paid search: too expensive for our price point at this stage.
- A referral programme: launched too early, before enough users had a
  reason to recommend us.
- Cold outreach: a low single-digit reply rate, not worth the time.

## The honest numbers

| Channel | Approx. share of first 1,000 | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Word of mouth, from support replies | ~20% | Time only |
| Comparison pages (organic search) | ~35% | Time only |
| Existing network, direct asks | ~25% | Time only |
| Launch day | ~3% | Time only |
| Everything else | ~17% | Mixed |

None of this scales the way paid acquisition does, which is the honest
trade-off: slower, but it cost us almost nothing beyond the hours we
would have spent anyway building the product.
